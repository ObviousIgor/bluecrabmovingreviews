How a flat-rate move works

Hourly movers can only estimate. A flat-rate mover prices the job itself, so the number on your quote is the number you pay.

Tell them what's moving

The size of your home, your furniture list, both addresses and your date. Mention stairs, elevators and long carries now.

Get one price

You get a written quote for that inventory and that work, before move day. Not a rate per hour, not a guess.

Move without the clock

If traffic is bad or the elevator is slow, the move takes longer but your price stays the same.

Flat-rate moving questions

Do movers in DC charge by the hour or a flat rate?

Many DC-area movers charge by the hour, so the final bill depends on traffic, parking and how long the elevator takes. Blue Crab and Red Cat both quote a flat rate instead: one price for your inventory and the work you describe, set before move day.

How is a flat rate worked out?

From the size of your home, your furniture and box list, both addresses and your move date. Tell the mover about stairs, elevators, long walks to the truck and anything heavy or fragile when you ask for the quote, so it is in the price.

Why are the lowest prices on weekdays early in the month?

Most leases end at the end of the month, and many people move on weekends, so those days book up first. Both companies publish their starting prices for weekday moves in the first three weeks of the month.

Should I get a quote from both?

Yes. Quotes from both companies are free, and comparing two flat rates for the same inventory shows you exactly what each one includes. Ask each one what is in the price: packing, materials, storage and any extra stops.
